## Procedure and method-specific results

1. Establish the shared system drawing or equivalent text model that participants can see. Record `shared-model` with system/evidence references, the caller's deck revision/access statement in `deck-access`, and participant pseudonyms in `player:<key>`. Type the access statement as `claim` evidence with purpose `description`. An observation that someone supplied it supports attribution only; a supported `deck-access` fact must say whose claim it records and leave lawfulness unverified. Preserve a mismatch between supplied deck revision and the pinned source as a method gap; do not silently call another variant this one.
2. Facilitate the cheat sheet's setup within the source scope. Ask participants to set aside skepticism for 15 minutes and record that instruction as `setup:skepticism`. State the goal of winning hands by connecting a card's threat to the shared system, without adding scoring rules; record `setup:goal`. Hand out one deck per small group, remove the instruction, strategy and threat-list cards, and shuffle the threat cards; record `setup`. Draw a random demonstration card, connect it to the shared model, then have the next clockwise player take a same-suit turn; record `demonstration`. Point participants to their deck's strategy and play flowcharts and record `setup:references`. Use lawful deck instructions for further game rules. In an existing transcript, record what occurred rather than instructing it again. Each setup fact and the demonstration need explicit supplied participation evidence; missing any of these steps leaves setup partial with a named gap and prevents completed facilitation. Do not reconstruct a shuffled hand or infer an unrecorded step from a generic statement that setup happened.
3. For each actual recorded turn, link the player's own system scenario to the shared model, their caller-local card handle/suit, prerequisites and uncertainty. Record `turn:<key>` facts linking the player-contribution evidence and any resulting threat. The handle is transcript provenance, not a verified taxonomy mapping. Preserve whether the session was collaborative or competitive only when supplied. Do not invent turns to fill suits, roles or hands.
4. Keep notes on supported threat hypotheses and protective alternatives. Record `turn-counterevidence:<key>` and preserve differing opinions without inventing votes or consensus. Card selection is a brainstorming cue, not proof of vulnerability. Preserve actual game decisions as attributed source facts; do not turn them into risk acceptance or an organization-approved treatment.
5. Record proposed outcomes, verification, coverage and unplayed/unavailable areas. Use `facilitation-scope` and `workshop-completion-basis` to distinguish a completed bounded recorded round from a whole game, full STRIDE assessment or solo analysis. `applied` records actual phases in order: `shared-model-and-deck`, `setup-and-demonstration`, `recorded-card-to-system-turns`, `counterevidence-and-notes`, `outcomes-and-coverage`. Sufficient actual records may complete bounded facilitation; missing actual player input makes the workshop partial/not-assessed, even if useful individual hypotheses are possible.
