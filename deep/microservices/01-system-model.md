## Work

Build the system around business operations and authority transitions, not just deployment boxes. Model each supplied service as an asset and each distinct workload principal or human as an actor. Preserve the stated link between a workload and its identity in related_refs and descriptions; do not merge all services into one trusted actor. Inventory resource records, reservations, operation outcomes and downstream messages as data with their supplied ownership and sensitivity.

Represent each directed service call, reply and asynchronous delivery separately where supplied. A flow description records the operation, initiating actor/tenant context, receiving principal and trust decision; do not add custom fields. Flow endpoints must be assets, actors or entry_points; states and zones are not valid endpoints. Boundary sides are zones or assets. Dependencies can link any system record and can connect a transaction state to the service whose result it awaits. Unknown endpoints remain null with an open topology gap.

Model the supplied pending, completed, failed and unknown outcomes, resource reservations, cancellation and compensation only where the context defines them. Use system states and dependencies with evidence, not an invented state machine or attack graph. Describe what condition permits each transition and who can request a retry/replay. Keep transport reachability distinct from business permission, and authorization separate from evidence of workload authentication.

Map operator paths separately from customer traffic. For mixed microservices/cloud-control-plane evidence, give the control-to-workload boundary its supplied owner, preserve globally shared service/principal/data IDs, and keep microservices ownership for service-operation boundaries. If ownership or identity matching is ambiguous, record an open identity gap rather than choosing by label. Capture controls at their supplied status and requirements as supplied or proposed; a documented protection is not a passed test. Return TM_SYSTEM_MODEL with an exposure map grounded in its typed records; do not enumerate threats yet.

### Microservices boundary and lifecycle checks

Apply these checks to the supplied system, with coverage tied to actual system IDs.

1. **Workload identity:** distinguish the process, its runtime identity and the human or service that initiated an operation. Trace identity issuance, replacement, expiry and revocation where evidence exists. An authenticated peer is not automatically entitled to every operation.
2. **Direct reachability and operation authority:** follow a compromised low-privilege service to a higher-privilege operation. Separate a reachable network route from an accepted business request. Identify the decision point and supplied restrictions on that exact operation.
3. **Chained delegation:** trace the original user, tenant, resource and requested action through upstream → intermediate → downstream service or another supplied chain. Identify where context is retained, narrowed, replaced or lost; distinguish a service acting for a user from one acting on its own authority. Do not treat a gateway check as proof for subsequent hops.
4. **Distributed ownership:** identify who owns each resource, reservation and operation result and downstream work decision. Trace which service may change each and the caller's business invariant across them. A local success does not establish completion of the whole transaction.
5. **Retries and partial outcomes:** distinguish failure before a side effect, success with a lost reply, and an unknown outcome. Follow duplicate, delayed and reordered work, changed intent using an old transaction identity, cancellation racing completion, and compensation. Ask which state authorizes the next effect and what evidence links it to the same transaction.
6. **Recovery and availability:** trace dependency outage, retry amplification and recovery against supplied capacity or recovery objectives. Inspect who may restart or replay work and how participating services reconcile their state. Examine whether a receiving service accepts a protected operation while required token validation or a policy dependency is unavailable, or a cached decision is stale under supplied freshness or revocation requirements. Unknown behavior is an evidence gap, not a demonstrated bypass. Missing limits are gaps; do not invent a denial of service or numeric threshold.
7. **Operators and control plane:** separate business authorization from authority to change identities, routes, service configuration and evidence collection. In a mixed cloud-control-plane scope, retain one global identity per shared actor/service and one owner per boundary. A deployment right alone does not prove access to customer data.
8. **Attribution and data exposure:** follow request and transaction attribution through messages, service logs and support views, including delayed or failed actions. Record which sensitive fields are exposed to which principals. Correlation text is not authorization, and several views of one event are not independent observations.

For each check, preserve protective evidence at its actual scope. A service mesh, network segment, gateway, signed assertion or retry label alone establishes neither a weakness nor effectiveness. Unsupported areas get gaps or partial/not_assessed coverage. The three scenario families are analysis targets, never mandatory defect findings: downstream privilege, authority/context through a chain, and transaction integrity during retries or partial failure.

### Record types used here

The compact dictionary below is the field contract, not example output. All listed fields are required and no extras are allowed. `string` is nonempty; `text` may be empty; `scalar` means string, number, boolean or null. `id` follows the identity rule above. `refs` is a distinct array of IDs; `strings` is a distinct array of strings. Arrays remain arrays even for one item and have distinct values unless marked ordered. Preserve untouched records using their supplied typed shape. Empty arrays mean no records, not proof of assessment; record coverage explicitly.


- Inventory: `id:id`, `locator:string`, `access:read|partial|skipped|unavailable`, `coverage:string`, `reason:string|null`. Read/partial items go in `processed_inputs`; skipped/unavailable items go in `unprocessed_inputs`. Record a partially read input once and describe its unread portion.
- Attachment: `id:id`, `kind:prior_model|method_fragment|risk_rubric|evidence|legacy|other`, `origin:string`, `access:available|partial|unavailable`, `namespace:string`, `content:opaque source JSON`, `locator:string|null`, `limitations:text`, `redaction:{status:applied|none_found|not_performed,notes:strings}`. Available means portable content is actually included. Opaque content preserves source data; it is not typed model content. Before writing or displaying it, inspect and sanitize the carried content: use applied with nonempty notes naming location, kind and placeholder, never the removed secret, or none_found only after inspection. Do not emit portable content marked not_performed. Unsanitized imported content is marked not_performed; review and sanitize it before handoff rather than merely flipping the label. If inspection is unavailable, omit sensitive/unread content and record unavailable access and the preservation limit.
- Evidence: `id:id`, `locator:string`, `source_kind:repository|document|diagram|test_result|caller|other`, `source_revision:string|null`, `date:string|null`, `statement:string`, `status:observation|claim|proposal`, `purpose:description|implementation|effectiveness|execution|decision|resolution`, `access:read|partial`, `limitations:text`, `excerpt:text|null`, `derived_from:refs`. Derivation links evidence and cannot cycle; repeated summaries are not independent observations.
- Coverage: `id:id`, `target_ref:id|null`, `method_ref:id|null`, `lens:string|null`, `status:assessed|partial|not_assessed|not_applicable`, `reason:string`. Target is a system record or null for scope-wide coverage; method ref is an execution record, not a name. At least method_ref or lens is non-null; assessed method coverage requires a completed execution.
- Gap: `id:id`, `kind:evidence|topology|method|technology|identity|coverage|conversion`, `description:string`, `needed:string`, `affected_refs:refs`, `status:open|closed`, `evidence_refs:refs`. Closing a gap requires observation evidence.
- Element: `id:id`, `kind:string`, `description:string`, `basis:observed|claimed|proposed_design`, `evidence_refs:refs`, `rationale:string`, `related_refs:refs`. Used for assets, actors, data, zones, entry points and states. Observed/claimed elements link corresponding evidence; proposed designs state their premise. Related refs point to system records.

Endpoints reference assets/actors/entry points; data and boundaries reference their respective collections.
- Dependency: Flow fields except `data_refs` and `boundary_refs`; endpoints may reference any system records.
- Control: `id:id`, `description:string`, `affected_refs:refs`, `threat_refs:refs`, `status:claimed|designed|implemented|tested`, `evidence_refs:refs`, `limitations:text`, `verification_refs:refs`, `architecture_ids:strings`.
