## API scope and boundary checks

Use this prompt for the supplied API/service-interface scope; include architecture identity `api` as this requested lens, preserving other caller-selected and carried architectures. Do not infer OAuth, a token format, a gateway product, a transport delivery guarantee or a deployment stack from the word API. Apply `identity-federation` only to supplied issuer, subject, client, audience and resource relationships. Share global identities across sets, give each boundary one evidenced architecture owner, and preserve all contributing architecture IDs on shared threats and controls. An unknown owner stays null with an open identity gap.

For a source_model_id/source_namespace/source_id/source_collection, keep its one existing identity disposition across newer source revisions. Preserve the mapping ID, original source revision/provenance and existing evidence; append corroborating evidence when supplied, not another per-revision alias. Distinct source snapshots get distinct stable sanitized attachments. A repeated import of the same snapshot adds neither another mapping nor independent evidence. Preserve unresolved identity alternatives instead of merging by label. Preserve exact supplied numeric values, nulls and IDs; do not round source values or normalize ratings. Attachments, including prior-changes history snapshots, are envelope additions, not addressable Change targets.

Check the following only within this stage's task: collect evidence in scope, map relationships in modeling, develop scenarios in analysis, challenge premises in review, propose responses in treatment, and report actual status in handoff. Do not perform later stages merely to fill this list.

1. Follow the caller's identity and intended resource audience through admission at the gateway and authorization in the backend. Distinguish the client from the resource owner and delegated actor where supplied. Recognition of a caller does not establish permission on every object or operation. Preserve issuer/subject scope at identity translation; equal local subject strings need not denote the same principal.
2. Trace a changed object identifier through individual reads, updates, bulk selection, asynchronous export execution and result retrieval. Bind each object's owner, the requested action and the requesting principal at the relevant decision point. A passing GET check proves nothing about export selection or download. Identify what authority is preserved or reconsidered when work is delayed, ownership changes or access is revoked.
3. Separate object authority, function authority and property authority. An allowed resource read does not authorize a privileged operation, a write to its business state or disclosure of every returned property. Follow caller-supplied fields and backend-returned fields to their actual use; identify which fields the caller may read or change from supplied policy, not from guessed business rules.
4. Trace replay, duplicates, concurrent requests and operation order against explicit business invariants. For the supplied workflow, determine the permitted competing state transitions, commitment point and allowed number of effects only when supplied. Examine retry after an unknown outcome and recovery after partial processing. Separate credential replay from replay of a business operation; a unique request identifier alone establishes neither safe sequencing nor exactly-once processing.
5. Trace a caller-controlled callback destination from registration through storage, outbound-worker authority, destination checks and any redirects. Ask what destinations and redirect transitions are actually permitted, where that evidence applies and what the worker can reach. Presence of a configurable URL does not establish SSRF, network access or successful disclosure.
6. Follow the notification payload to its recipient and external response fields back to the worker and every downstream consumer. Separate the data authorized to leave from the authority granted to returned fields. A delivery acknowledgment may record notification receipt; it must not be assumed to authorize business-state changes. If a separate legitimate external status feed exists, map its distinct source, resource correlation and authority. Do not invent a relationship between the two channels.
7. Review bounded use and recovery of bulk exports and callback work: cancellation, delayed jobs, retained results, retries, slow destinations and oversized responses. Identify the supplied business cost or availability objective and any observed limits; do not invent quotas, retry policies or service guarantees. Compare normal and recovery paths, including evidence about output retention and access after revocation.
8. In mixed API/federation scope, trace issuer and subject to the global principal identity, then to resource ownership and asynchronous authority. Preserve API and federation contributions on the same threat/control when both support it. Audience, action scope and authenticated identity are distinct from the local ownership decision. Record unperformed optional identity-method work as coverage, never a completed procedure.

These are coverage lenses, not mandatory defects. Preserve scoped protective observations and counterexamples. A proposed architecture grounds hypotheses; implementation or execution observations are needed for evidenced weaknesses. Missing documents, role files, method fragments or a workshop deck do not block this baseline. Report performed lenses with null method_ref; a named branded method needs actual supplied procedure/execution evidence before it is reported as performed.

For technology rules, use only an exact, already resolved secure-coding pack the caller supplies for the stack, with its group/technology/version identity. Preserve resolver output/provenance; never guess an identity or hand-maintain a mapping. Without tools, files, a catalog or a matching pack, name the missing dependency and the resolver result the caller could supply, mark dependent rules unverified and continue architectural outcomes without waiting. Source URLs inform these questions, not the facts of the system under review.

### Record types used here

The compact dictionary below is the field contract, not example output. All listed fields are required and no extras are allowed. `string` is nonempty; `text` may be empty; `scalar` means string, number, boolean or null. `id` follows the identity rule above. `refs` is a distinct array of IDs; `strings` is a distinct array of strings. Arrays remain arrays even for one item and have distinct values unless marked ordered. Preserve untouched records using their supplied typed shape. Empty arrays mean no records, not proof of assessment; record coverage explicitly.


- Inventory: `id:id`, `locator:string`, `access:read|partial|skipped|unavailable`, `coverage:string`, `reason:string|null`. Read/partial items go in `processed_inputs`; skipped/unavailable items go in `unprocessed_inputs`. Record a partially read input once and describe its unread portion.
- Attachment: `id:id`, `kind:prior_model|method_fragment|risk_rubric|evidence|legacy|other`, `origin:string`, `access:available|partial|unavailable`, `namespace:string`, `content:opaque source JSON`, `locator:string|null`, `limitations:text`, `redaction:{status:applied|none_found|not_performed,notes:strings}`. Available means portable content is actually included. Opaque content preserves source data; it is not typed model content. Before writing or displaying it, inspect and sanitize the carried content: use applied with nonempty notes naming location, kind and placeholder, never the removed secret, or none_found only after inspection. Do not emit portable content marked not_performed. Unsanitized imported content is marked not_performed; review and sanitize it before handoff rather than merely flipping the label. If inspection is unavailable, omit sensitive/unread content and record unavailable access and the preservation limit.
- Evidence: `id:id`, `locator:string`, `source_kind:repository|document|diagram|test_result|caller|other`, `source_revision:string|null`, `date:string|null`, `statement:string`, `status:observation|claim|proposal`, `purpose:description|implementation|effectiveness|execution|decision|resolution`, `access:read|partial`, `limitations:text`, `excerpt:text|null`, `derived_from:refs`. Derivation links evidence and cannot cycle; repeated summaries are not independent observations.
- Coverage: `id:id`, `target_ref:id|null`, `method_ref:id|null`, `lens:string|null`, `status:assessed|partial|not_assessed|not_applicable`, `reason:string`. Target is a system record or null for scope-wide coverage; method ref is an execution record, not a name. At least method_ref or lens is non-null; assessed method coverage requires a completed execution.
- Gap: `id:id`, `kind:evidence|topology|method|technology|identity|coverage|conversion`, `description:string`, `needed:string`, `affected_refs:refs`, `status:open|closed`, `evidence_refs:refs`. Closing a gap requires observation evidence.

- Change: `id:id`, `kind:added|modified|unchanged|retired|reopened|merged|ambiguous`, `current_refs:refs`, `previous_ids:refs`, `rationale:string`, `evidence_refs:refs`. Previous IDs are historical, not live references. Compare only a supplied matching baseline; absence is not automatic retirement. Declare every non-unchanged delta against that baseline exactly once, with its actual kind and identities; do not claim reconciliation succeeded when changes were merely computed for a prospective declaration. Without a usable parent, leave changes empty; preserve supplied declarations only inside sanitized source history and state that baseline comparison was not run.
